The degree is French. The support around it usually isn't.

A Law with French Law degree, at UCL, Warwick, Newcastle or elsewhere, drops you into a French law faculty for a full year, French civil law, French constitutional law, French contract law, and the French exercises that go with them, the fiche d'arrêt, the commentaire d'arrêt, the cas pratique. None of these exist in an English law degree, and none of them get taught before you land in France.

Most students find out how different French legal method is only once they're sitting in front of a French professor who assumes everyone already knows how to structure a cas pratique. The French system rewards a very specific, very rigid way of answering a legal question, and getting it wrong costs marks even when the legal reasoning underneath is correct. On top of that, French lecturers rarely offer the kind of one-to-one office hours a UK student is used to, so a concept that doesn't click in the lecture theatre tends to stay unclear until the exam.

That's exactly the gap my tutoring fills. I go over the notion your French course didn't quite land, mark your practice answers before the same mistake repeats itself, and stay reachable on WhatsApp between sessions for a quick question before a deadline. I already work with students based in Dubai, Geneva, Brussels and Luxembourg, on top of students in France. The time zone changes, the method doesn't.
